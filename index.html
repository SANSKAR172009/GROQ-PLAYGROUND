From Logits to Perplexity: The Mathematical Pipeline

Understanding every stage in the forward inference pass of an autoregressive transformer.
1
Raw Model Logits
z = LM_Head(hL)
The final transformer layer produces an unnormalized score zv for every token v in the vocabulary |V|.
2
Softmax Probabilities
P(wi) = &frac;exp(zi / T)∑j exp(zj / T)
Exponentiating and normalizing turns raw arbitrary scores into a rigorous probability distribution that sums strictly to 1.0.
3
Surprisal / NLL Loss
NLLi = −ln P(wi | w<i)
Negative log-likelihood quantifies surprise in nats (or bits if log2). If probability is 100%, surprisal is 0. If probability is 1%, surprisal is 4.6 nats.
4
Exponentiated Mean Loss
PPL = exp(&frac;1;N ∑i=1N NLLi)
Perplexity is the exponent of the average cross-entropy loss. It represents the effective branching factor of the model's vocabulary choice.

Frequently Asked Questions & Intuitions

Key conceptual insights for students, AI developers, and machine learning researchers.
🎲 What is the "Dice Roll" analogy for Perplexity?
If a model has a perplexity of 10 on a given text, it was on average as confused as someone rolling a fair 10-sided die to guess the next word. A perplexity of 1 means the model was as certain as a 1-sided die (absolute 100% confidence).
🔍 How do AI detectors (GPTZero) use Perplexity?
Language models produce text that maximizes probability, resulting in consistently low perplexity with uniform pacing. Human writing, by contrast, has high burstiness: a mixture of predictable common phrases interspersed with unusual, creative, or surprising vocabulary spikes.
⚠️ Is Lower Perplexity Always Better?
Not necessarily! A degenerate model that repeats "the the the the the" achieves an artificially ultra-low perplexity score because each repetition is predictable. This is known as the repetition trap. Real evaluation requires balancing low perplexity with diversity and human alignment.
📏 Why can't we compare PPL across different tokenizers?
Perplexity is normalized per token, not per word. If Model A uses byte-level BPE with 100 tokens for a sentence and Model B uses word-level tokenization with 25 tokens, their denominators and vocabulary spaces differ completely. You can only compare perplexity across models that share the exact same tokenizer!
